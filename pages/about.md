## About the Collection

**Illustrating Paradise Lost: Engravings from the 1794–1796 Parsons Edition** is a digital exhibit presenting ten engraved illustrations from John Milton's *Paradise Lost* published by J. Parsons in London between 1794 and 1796.

The collection explores how eighteenth-century artists interpreted Milton's epic through images of creation, temptation, rebellion, redemption, and exile. Rather than merely illustrating the poem, these engravings provide visual interpretations of its theological and philosophical themes.

The exhibit demonstrates how descriptive metadata can organize historical images into a searchable scholarly collection. Each object includes publication information, artistic attribution, descriptive metadata, and alternative text to support accessibility and digital humanities research.

## Major Themes

This exhibit follows the narrative progression of *Paradise Lost*:

- Satan's rebellion
- Heavenly order
- Creation of Adam and Eve
- Innocence in Eden
- Temptation
- The Fall
- Expulsion from Paradise

Together these illustrations reveal how eighteenth-century readers encountered Milton's poem as both literature and visual culture.
